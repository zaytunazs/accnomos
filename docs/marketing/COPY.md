## How it works

Four steps.

1. Send it in. Photograph a receipt in the Android app, upload it on the web, or let receipts arrive from your email inbox.
2. The AI proposes. A Meta model reads the document and suggests supplier, category, VAT and totals. Code checks the arithmetic.
3. You approve. Each proposal waits in Review. Approve, edit or reject. Nothing posts on its own.
4. VAT and records, ready. Approved entries build your ledger, your VAT boxes and a spreadsheet copy of your books.

## Feature blocks

### Receipts from email and photos

Snap a receipt on your phone or let supplier emails be picked up from your inbox. The AI reads each one into a proposal you check. Example: a £60 plus VAT invoice from a fictional supplier, Brightfix Electrical Ltd, arrives by email and waits in Review with supplier, date and totals filled in.

### Bank lines, matched and coded

Import a bank statement as a CSV file and see each line matched to entries already in your books. Confirm the matches and deal with the rest one by one. Example: a fictional £240 payment to Brightfix Electrical Ltd matches the bill for the same amount dated four days earlier.

### VAT boxes, worked out by code

Your nine VAT boxes are calculated by code from approved entries, not typed by hand. Connect to HMRC through the sandbox flow and walk through obligations, checks and a declaration. Example: a fictional quarter for Harthill Bakery Ltd shows £1,240 in Box 1 and £310 in Box 4.

### Income Tax for the self-employed

If you are self-employed, quarterly Income Tax figures are worked out from your approved entries, with the ledger lines behind each box. The HMRC connection here is sandbox only. Example: a fictional sole trader, Priya Nair, sees £18,400 of turnover for the period to 5 July with each line traceable.

### Pay runs, estimated in pence

Enter hours and rates to get a pay-run estimate with tax and National Insurance shown line by line. These are estimates for planning, not payroll filings. Example: a fictional employee, Tom Ellis, on 20 hours at £13.50 an hour gets a gross of £270 with deductions listed beside it.

### Voice and chat that wait

Ask a question in plain words, typed or spoken, and get an answer with figures checked against your books. Short instructions become cards you approve first. Example: "Andy did 2 hours overtime at double time on the Hendon job" becomes a timesheet card for the Hendon job, and nothing is recorded until you tap Approve.

### Month-end checks, listed plainly

Run a month-end check that lists missing statements, uncategorised costs, possible duplicates and anything dated into a closed month. Work through the list and re-close when done. Example: fictional March books for Bramblewood Joinery Ltd flag two unmatched bank lines and one receipt still sitting in the inbox.

### Your books live in OneDrive

Your ledger, documents and spreadsheets are files in your own OneDrive folder. Accnomos keeps no database of your books. Example: disconnect and the fictional folder for Bramblewood Joinery Ltd, with its ledger events and Books.xlsx, is still sitting in your OneDrive.

## For accountants

Invite your accountant to your books. They open the same files you see: the ledger, Books.xlsx, reports and the audit trail. Queries stay simple. Anything they question can be undone with a reversing entry, never a delete, so the record stays complete.

## Trust

AI proposes, you approve. Every figure that reaches your books has passed code checks in whole pence, and a second independent reading checks the first on key documents. There is no database of your books on our side: the files live in your OneDrive. Every approve, reject, match, payment and undo is written to an audit trail you can open.

## FAQ

1. How much does it cost. Pricing is shown in the app before you pay for anything. The demo with sample books costs nothing to try.
2. Where are my books kept. In your own OneDrive, as files you can open. We keep no database of your books.
3. Does it file to HMRC. The HMRC connection in the product today is sandbox only, for VAT and for self-employed Income Tax. It is there to practise the full flow, not to file a live return.
4. Which banks are covered. You import bank statements as CSV files. Barclays, Starling, Tide and Monzo style layouts are read, along with HSBC and Lloyds style paid-in and paid-out columns.
5. Can I use it on my phone. There is an Android app with capture, Review, Ask, VAT and reports. Everyday admin such as bank uploads and detailed settings stays on the web.
6. Can I switch from Xero or QuickBooks. Export your lists and statements from your current software and bring them across as files. Your opening position is entered once, and new documents flow in from there.
7. Does my accountant need an account. You invite your accountant to see your books. They read the same ledger, spreadsheet and reports that you see.
8. What can the AI get wrong. It can misread a total, a date or a VAT number, most often on crumpled photos or faint print. That is why every reading is checked by code and waits for your approval before it posts.
9. How do I stop. Export your files first if you wish, then disconnect. Your books are already files in your OneDrive, so nothing is locked in.
10. Is my data used to train models. No. Your documents are read to keep your books, and nothing else.
